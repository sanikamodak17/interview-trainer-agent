# Interview Preparation Knowledge Base

## Section 1: Role-Specific Technical Interview Questions

### Software Engineer / Software Developer

#### Entry Level (0–2 years)
- Explain the difference between a stack and a queue.
- What is Object-Oriented Programming (OOP)? List its four principles.
- What is the difference between == and === in JavaScript?
- Explain the concept of recursion with an example.
- What is the time complexity of binary search?
- What is a linked list? How does it differ from an array?
- Explain what REST API is and its key principles.
- What is Git and why is version control important?
- How does garbage collection work in Java/Python?
- What is the difference between SQL and NoSQL databases?

#### Mid Level (3–5 years)
- Explain microservices architecture vs. monolithic architecture.
- What design patterns have you used, and when?
- Describe your experience with CI/CD pipelines.
- How do you handle concurrency and race conditions in code?
- What is the CAP theorem in distributed systems?
- Explain dependency injection and inversion of control.
- What are your strategies for code review?
- How do you approach system design for a URL shortener?
- Explain the differences between Docker and Kubernetes.
- How do you ensure the security of an API?

#### Senior Level (6+ years)
- How would you design a scalable distributed cache?
- Describe an architectural decision you regret and what you learned.
- How do you approach technical debt in legacy systems?
- Design a ride-sharing backend system from scratch.
- What strategies do you use to mentor junior engineers?
- How do you decide between building vs. buying software components?
- Explain eventual consistency and how you handle conflicts.
- How would you design a real-time notification system?

### Data Scientist / Data Analyst

#### Entry Level
- What is the difference between supervised and unsupervised learning?
- Explain overfitting and how to prevent it.
- What is the bias-variance tradeoff?
- What is a confusion matrix?
- How do you handle missing values in a dataset?
- Explain the difference between mean, median, and mode.
- What is cross-validation?
- Describe the k-nearest neighbors (KNN) algorithm.

#### Mid Level
- How do you choose between logistic regression and a random forest?
- Explain gradient descent and its variants.
- What are hyperparameters and how do you tune them?
- How do you evaluate the performance of a regression model?
- Explain the difference between bagging and boosting.
- How do you handle class imbalance?
- What is PCA and when would you use it?
- Describe a feature engineering technique you've applied.

#### Senior Level
- How would you design an end-to-end ML pipeline for production?
- What is A/B testing and how do you design a statistically valid experiment?
- How do you ensure model fairness and mitigate bias?
- Describe a model monitoring strategy for a deployed ML system.

- Describe your process for gathering user requirements.
- Tell me about a product launch that did not go as planned.
- How do you measure the success of a product feature?
- What metrics would you track for a subscription-based SaaS product?

- How do you build a content strategy from scratch?
- What metrics do you use to track digital marketing performance?

## Section 2: Behavioral Interview Questions (STAR Method)

### Leadership and Influence
- Tell me about a time you led a team through a challenging project.
- Describe a situation where you had to influence without authority.
- Give an example of when you had to make a difficult decision with limited data.
- Tell me about a time you mentored someone. What was the outcome?

### Conflict Resolution
- Describe a conflict with a colleague and how you resolved it.
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager. How did you handle it?
- Give an example of a time a project was going off track. What did you do?

### Problem Solving
- Describe the most complex problem you've solved at work.
- Tell me about a time you identified and fixed a critical bug or error.
- Walk me through your process when approaching an ambiguous challenge.

### Adaptability and Learning
- Tell me about a time you had to learn a new technology or skill quickly.
- Describe a time when project requirements changed midway. How did you adapt?
- Give an example of a failure and what you learned from it.

### Teamwork and Collaboration
- Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for your team.
- Describe a successful cross-functional collaboration.
- How do you handle working with difficult team members?

## Section 3: Industry Expectations and HR Guidelines

### What Hiring Managers Look For

**Technical Roles**: Problem-solving ability, coding clarity, system design understanding, debugging skills, clean code principles, knowledge of tools and frameworks.

**Managerial Roles**: Leadership style, strategic thinking, stakeholder management, data-driven decision making, empathy and communication.

**All Roles**: Culture fit, growth mindset, communication skills, ownership and accountability, ability to handle feedback.

---

### Interview Formats by Company Type

#### FAANG / Big Tech (Google, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, Apple)
- Coding interviews: LeetCode-style problems (Easy to Hard)
- System design interviews: Scale-oriented distributed systems
- Behavioral interviews: Leadership Principles (Amazon), Googleyness
- Domain/role-specific deep dives

#### Startups
- Emphasis on culture fit and adaptability
- Take-home assignments or practical coding tasks
- Broad role expectations (wear many hats)
- Fast, fewer rounds

#### Enterprise/Consulting
- Case studies or business problem solving
- Client-facing presentation skills
- Process and compliance orientation
- Structured competency-based interviews

## Section 4: Model Answers & Improvement Tips

### STAR Method Framework
**S** – Situation: Set the context  
**T** – Task: Describe your responsibility  
**A** – Action: Explain what you specifically did  
**R** – Result: Share measurable outcomes  

**Tip**: Always quantify results where possible. "Reduced latency by 40%" is stronger than "made the system faster."

---

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Vague answers**: Always use specific examples with metrics.
2. **Negative talk about previous employers**: Stay professional.
3. **No questions asked**: Always prepare 3–5 thoughtful questions for the interviewer.
4. **Rambling**: Practice concise 90-second answers for behavioral questions.
5. **No research done**: Know the company's products, culture, and recent news.
6. **Overconfidence or underconfidence**: Balance your confidence with humility.

---

### Top Questions to Ask the Interviewer
- What does success look like in this role in the first 90 days?
- How would you describe the team culture?
- What are the biggest challenges facing the team right now?
- How does this role contribute to the company's broader mission?
- What professional growth opportunities exist within this company?

## Section 6: Salary Negotiation and Offer Evaluation

### Negotiation Tips
- Research market rates using Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, Levels.fyi.
- Always let the employer make the first offer.
- Negotiate total compensation: base salary, equity, bonus, benefits.
- Be polite but confident; use competing offers as leverage.

### Evaluating an Offer
- Base salary vs. market rate
- Equity/stock options (vesting schedule, cliff)
- Health, dental, and vision insurance
- Remote/flexible work policies
- PTO, parental leave, 401(k) matching
- Learning and development budget

## Section 7: Interview Preparation Strategies

### Two Weeks Before
- Research the company's products, competitors, funding, and culture.
- Review the job description carefully and map your skills.
- Prepare 5–7 STAR stories from your experience.
- Practice coding problems (for technical roles) on LeetCode, HackerRank.

### One Week Before
- Do mock interviews with friends or on Pramp, Interviewing.io.
- Review your resume line-by-line; be ready to explain everything.
- Prepare your "tell me about yourself" 2-minute pitch.
- Prepare thoughtful questions for interviewers.

### Day Before
- Rest and sleep well.
- Lay out your outfit or test your video call setup.
- Review key points, not new material.

### Day Of
- Arrive/log in 5–10 minutes early.
- Bring copies of your resume (in-person) or have it open (virtual).
- Take a moment to breathe and focus before the interview starts.
- Listen carefully; it's okay to pause and think before answering.
